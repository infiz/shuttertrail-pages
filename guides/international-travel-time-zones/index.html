International travel guide

Geotag photos accurately across time zones.

A photo and a GPS point can describe the same instant using different local times. The UTC offset keeps them aligned as your journey moves between time zones.

Local camera time is only part of the story

Imagine taking a photo at 9:00 a.m. in California and another at 9:00 a.m. after traveling to New York. The clock readings look identical, but they describe different instants. A UTC offset such as −08:00 or −05:00 supplies the missing time-zone context.

Why international travel makes this harder

Camera clocks usually display local time. After a flight or border crossing, changing the clock without preserving the correct time-zone offset—or leaving it unchanged without accounting for that choice—can move a photo hours away from the correct GPS point.

How the Shutter Trail workflow handles offsets

  1. The GPX track provides timestamped locations in an unambiguous time format.
  2. The Lightroom plug-in reads each photo’s capture time and embedded UTC offset using ExifTool.
  3. Each photo is converted to a comparable instant before the nearest track point is selected.
  4. If an offset is missing, the plug-in asks what to use and can apply that choice by photo or camera.

Tip: When you arrive in a new time zone, update the camera’s local time and time-zone setting together. Correct source metadata makes every later match easier to audit.

Review ambiguous matches

No automated workflow should hide uncertainty. Review photos missing offsets, matches far from a track point, and images captured around daylight-saving or international time-zone changes. If an offset is missing, the plug-in asks what to use before matching the photo.
