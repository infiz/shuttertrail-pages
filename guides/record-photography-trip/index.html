On-location guide

How to record a photography trip on iPhone.

Your camera does not need built-in GPS to preserve a trip’s locations. Record where you travel, keep shooting normally, and connect the photo and location timelines afterward.

Before the shoot

  1. Confirm Shutter Trail has the location access needed for the recording mode you plan to use.
  2. Check that your cameras show the correct local time and time zone.
  3. Start recording before the first photo, while you still have a moment to verify it.

Choose the right recording mode

Regular recording is appropriate when you want to conserve battery and can keep the app active as needed. Turn on High Accuracy when consistent background coverage matters more, such as during a hike or a fast-moving shoot. Turn it off when the session ends.

Record without internet

Shutter Trail records GPS locations without an internet connection, so you can keep building a trail in remote areas or while traveling without cellular service. Map imagery may depend on available or previously cached map data, but trail recording itself continues offline.

Keep the phone with the photographer

The recorded location describes the phone’s position. Keep it with the person carrying the cameras rather than leaving it in a vehicle or camera bag at base camp.

Create the trip afterward

Shutter Trail lets you define the trip time range after recording. That means you can capture a full day, then create a focused Trip containing only the part relevant to the photo session.

Export and archive the GPX file

Export the Trip as GPX and store it with the corresponding photo project. A descriptive filename and a folder beside the original photographs make the location record easy to find years later.

Tip: On long trips, export a GPX file at the end of each day. Smaller daily files are easier to organize and verify.
